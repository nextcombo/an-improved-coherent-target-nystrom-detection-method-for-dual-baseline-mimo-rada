# An Improved Coherent Target Nystrom Detection Method for Dual-Baseline MIMO Radar Based on Spatial Smoothing with MATLAB Codes

Introduction: The demanding precision and resolution of target positioning in radar systems have led to the research on Multiple Input Multiple Output (MIMO) radar over the past decade (Haimovich, Blum, & Cimini, 2008). A key feature of MIMO radar is its ability to simultaneously use multiple antennas to transmit orthogonal waveforms and receive the same reflected signals using multiple receiving antennas. Theoretical studies indicate that MIMO radar has significant advantages in suppressing noise, resisting fading effects, enhancing parameter resolvability, and improving spatial resolution compared to traditional phased array radars (Augusto, Antonio De, & Huang, 2016; Bekkerman & Tabrikian, 2006; Cui, Li, & Rangaswamy, 2014), surpassing conventional phased array radars. One major issue attracting extensive research in parameter recognition is the estimation of target angles (Chen, Zheng, Wang, Li, & Wu, 2017; Wen, Xiong, Su, & Zhang, 2017; Xu, Li, & Stoica, 2008; Zhang, Yin, Chen, Gao, & Ansari, 2012).
The subspace-based methods, such as the ESPRIT algorithm, MUSIC algorithm, Capon algorithm, and PARAFAC algorithm, have been proven to be effective tools for multi-objective angle estimation. As a result, they have been developed and applied to estimate the departure direction (DOD) and arrival direction (DOA) of targets (Chen, Shao, & Chen, 2019; Duofang, Baixiao, & Guodong, 2008; Nion & Sidiropoulos, 2009; Yan, Li, & Liao, 2008; Zhang, Xu, Xu, & Xu, 2010). The ESPRIT method is considered a high-resolution and computationally efficient approach that requires an antenna array with displacement invariance to exploit its invariance. Consequently, as a result, in contrast to searching on the spatial spectrum, direct computation of parameters is achieved. In Oh et al. (2015), the DOD/DOA estimation was obtained by applying the relationship between the transmitting and receiving arrays of the ESPRIT method, achieving automatic pairing. Then Yeo, Chung, Yang, Kim and Chung (2017) proposed a two-dimensional MUSIC algorithm to estimate the DOD and DOA of dual-baseline MIMO radars. Another consideration is the propagation subspace method, which obtains its angle estimates without performing the eigen decomposition of the data signal matrix. Zheng and Zhang (2011) applied this method to multi-objective angle estimation in MIMO radar.
The listed angle estimation algorithms assume that the signal to be estimated is non-coherent. This paper investigates the problem of angle estimation for coherent sources in dual-baseline MIMO radar. In reality, in most cases, coherent signals arise due to multipath propagation or intelligent interference (Cowell and Freear, 2010). Multipath signals occur when signals from a specific target travel through different paths with the same Doppler frequency and their radar cross-sections are correlated random variables. As a result, when these algorithms are exposed to coherent sources, their signal covariance matrices lead to a loss of rank, resulting in a significant degradation in performance. To address this issue, various preprocessing techniques have been proposed to de-correlate the signals. These preprocessing techniques include matrix reconstruction methods (Shu and Zhao, 2016; Zheng, Zhang, and Wang, 2013), spatial smoothing methods (SS) (Chen, 1997; Cintagunta and Palanisamy, 2018; Hong, Wan, Cheng, and Ke, 2015; Hong, Wan, and Ke, 2015; Li, Chen, and Dai, 2016; Pillai and Kwon, 1989; Zhang, Liu, Wang, & Wu, 2014), as well as joint spatial and polarization smoothing methods (Rahamim, Tabrikian, and Shavit, 2004; Zheng and Wu, 2016). These techniques generally produce de-correlated signals, while matrix reconstruction methods avoid spectrum searching while ensuring array gain, but they are more complex in special environments, especially when operating under conditions where the array structure is continuously moving. Additionally, spatial smoothing methods suffer from a loss of dimensionality, leading to an array gain loss. Furthermore, these attempts to preprocess the signals add additional complex computations, ultimately leading to heavy computational loads. For example, polarimetric smoothing methods are computationally unattractive due to the search in two dimensions.
The Nyström approximation, originating from the field of integral equation, is a method to ensure reduced storage requirements and improve the computational complexity of matrix-based operations on complex datasets (Baker, 1977; Williams & Seeger, 2001). In recent years, it has gained widespread popularity in spectral clustering and further developed normalized cuts and geometric harmonics among other spectral methods (Belongie et al., 2002).
In this paper, we propose a compact preprocessing technique to estimate the benefits of dual-baseline MIMO radar with joint DOD and DOA coherent sources as a lower computational attractiveness and aperture gain. After matching filtering, the technique initially disrupts the ill-structured nature of the obtained signal by developing modified spatial smoothing techniques that ensure the averaged spatial smoothing covariance matrices for all antennas while maintaining the low-rank kernel matrices generated from the obtained signal. The data matrix is based on the Nyström formulation block. Then, more accurate estimates of the signal subspace are obtained using an eigenvalue decomposition (EVD) method, where the DoD and DOA pair estimation is realized using the ESPRIT method. Additionally, due to its high resolution capability, the method can distinguish more targets at low SNR levels. The effectiveness of the new method is validated through mathematical simulations.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces the data model of the dual-baseline MIMO radar, followed by the algorithm development in Section 3, which includes the reconstruction of the data matrix's preprocessing of the coherent data matrix through modified spatial smoothing techniques. Section 4 provides a detailed analysis of the algorithm, and Section 5 presents numerical simulation results and performance analysis. The paper concludes in Section 6.

Conclusion This paper investigates the performance degradation due to the coherence of the received signal sources and the high computational burden caused by the application of complex angle estimation preprocessing techniques in dual-base MIMO radar. Unlike existing methods, this approach ensures that signal decoupling and dimensionality loss are accommodated within a much lower computational data matrix. This is achieved through adapting spatial smoothing methods based on formulated low-rank data matrix blocks using the Nyström approximation. Subsequently, the potential angles of targets are estimated using subspace translation variance auto-pairing technology. Numerical simulation results indicate that the proposed scheme's performance and efficiency surpass those of competing technologies. However, further research will focus on the direction finding for closely spaced dual-base MIMO arrays with low-angle coherent targets.
